Abstract 

Physical AI models such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) archi-tectures enable generalist robotic policies through large-scale trans-former backbones and diffusion-based action decoders. While edge GPU platforms excel at parallelizing the compute-intensive vision-transformer workloads, they exhibit fundamental limitations for the Diffusion Action Expert (DAE) module: the iterative denoising process requires repeated parameter loading from DRAM across multiple steps, resulting in memory-bound performance where the GPU’s massive computational throughput remains underutilized. This mismatch between DAE’s I/O-intensive characteristics and GPU’s compute-centric architecture motivates a heterogeneous acceleration approach. This paper presents EdgeDAE , a heteroge-neous FPGA-GPU system that strategically partitions workloads based on computational characteristics. We offload the perception-heavy vision-transformer to GPU while accelerating DAE infer-ence on FPGA through complete on-chip parameter storage in BRAM/URAM. This architecture eliminates the memory bottleneck by co-designing quantization strategies, fixed-point arithmetic, and hardware-efficient random number generation for the FPGA fabric. Compared to an edge GPU baseline, EdgeDAE reduces end-to-end inference latency by 52.5% for Octo-Small and 38.7% for Octo-Base, with up to 2 .10 × higher throughput; compared to a consumer GPU (RTX 4090), it achieves ∼17 × higher energy efficiency.

1 Introduction 

Physical AI enables robots to understand and interact with the physical world through embodied intelligence [18]. Central to this advance are Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models [2, 17, 21], which integrate visual perception, language understanding, and action generation into unified transformer architectures. By pre-training on large-scale robot trajectory datasets such as Open X-Embodiment with over 800K demonstrations across diverse tasks and morphologies [12], VLA models achieve remarkable generaliza-tion capabilities, enabling zero-shot transfer to new tasks, objects, and environments through natural language instructions or visual goal specifications. Despite their impressive capabilities, deploying VLA models on edge platforms remains challenging due to their substantial computational and memory requirements. While recent efforts have introduced compact variants—such as the Octo model family achieving orders-of-magnitude size reduction compared to earlier VLA models—real-time inference still demands high-end consumer-grade GPUs. For instance, Octo-Small achieves 17 it/s and Octo-Base 13 it/s on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU [17], consuming approximately 200 W of power [9]. Such power and cost constraints preclude deployment on mobile manipulation platforms, warehouse robots, and field scenarios with strict power, thermal, and cost limitations. The Octo architecture exposes a workload partitioning opportu-nity. Its vision-language transformer is compute-intensive and well matched to an edge GPU, while the Diffusion Action Expert (DAE) is a compact MLP-based diffusion policy [4, 6] executed repeatedly across denoising steps. Despite containing only a small fraction of total model parameters, the DAE becomes a bottleneck on GPUs because iterative sampling reloads parameters from DRAM and leaves wide GPU datapaths underutilized [3, 8]. The same compact parameter footprint makes the DAE suitable for FPGA deployment: all weights and diffusion constants can reside in BRAM/URAM, eliminating external memory traffic during the denoising loop. This motivates EdgeDAE’s heterogeneous partition: keep perception on GPU and offload the latency-sensitive DAE to FPGA. This paper presents EdgeDAE , a heterogeneous FPGA-GPU system that accelerates Octo VLA inference on edge platforms through co-designed hardware-software optimizations. We deploy EdgeDAE on a Jetson Orin Nano + Xilinx Kria KR260 heteroge-neous platform, where the Jetson handles perception-heavy vision-transformer processing while KR260 accelerates the DAE module. Our key contributions are: 

• Adaptive quantization decoupled from model scale: We de-velop per-channel INT8 and NF4-g16 mixed-precision [5] quan-tization strategies, each deployable across both Octo-Small and arXiv:2610.00311v1 [cs.AR] 28 Sep 2026 Zhiheng Chen, Ye Qiao, Mohammad Abdullah Al Faruque, and Sitao Huang 

Octo-Base to yield four hardware configurations. NF4-g16 halves URAM consumption relative to INT8—from 56 to 28 tiles on Octo-Small—while maintaining or improving action prediction quality. On Octo-Base, NF4-g16 achieves 6.84% lower mean squared error than the FP32 baseline through implicit regularization. 

• Highly-parallelized diffusion sampling architecture: We implement a parallel Gaussian random number generator pipeline with 4-way Xorshift128 states and Box-Muller transform for 20-step diffusion inference. A unified Quantized Linear Unit kernel with flattened loop structure supports both INT8 direct multiply-accumulate at 𝑃 =128 MACs/cycle and NF4 lookup-table-based dequantization at 𝑃 =64 MACs/cycle through dual-bank URAM/BRAM access, achieving II=1 pipelined throughput with decoupled SiLU activation. 

• End-to-end heterogeneous system integration: Offloading the I/O-bound DAE to KR260 FPGA at 250 MHz while retain-ing compute-bound vision-transformers on Jetson Orin Nano re-duces end-to-end latency by 52.5% for Octo-Small, from 104.18 ms to 49.50 ms with 2 .10 × throughput gain, and by 38.7% for Octo-Base, from 142.59 ms to 87.39 ms with 1 .63 × throughput gain. 2 Related Work 

Prior diffusion acceleration primarily targets large image-generation denoisers. GPU-oriented work reduces sampling overhead or ap-plies post-training quantization, but still assumes a high-bandwidth memory system for large U-Net or transformer denoisers [3, 8]. FPGA accelerators such as SDA [19] and Diff-Acc [16] improve energy efficiency for Stable Diffusion or unconditional diffusion models, yet their target networks are orders of magnitude larger than Octo’s DAE and therefore continue to rely on off-chip memory. Embodied-AI hardware systems such as Dadu-Corki [7] accelerate broader robot manipulation pipelines, but do not isolate the com-pact diffusion action head as a heterogeneous partitioning target. Beyond diffusion policies, a broader line of edge-FPGA accelerators targets efficient LLM and transformer inference [13, 14, 20]. EdgeDAE addresses a different operating point: the DAE is small enough for complete FPGA on-chip storage, but its repeated 20-step denoising loop is latency-critical on edge GPUs. This enables a specialized datapath with integrated Gaussian random number generation, quantized MLP execution, and precomputed diffusion constants.

3.2 Edge GPU vs. Edge FPGA 

This repeated denoising loop is poorly matched to an edge GPU. The Jetson Orin Nano integrates 1024 CUDA cores, 32 Tensor Cores, 8 GB LPDDR5, and a 2 MiB GPU L2 cache [10, 11]. The DAE foot-print reaches approximately 1.8 MB at INT8 precision, occupying nearly all nominal L2 capacity before activations and other work-ing data. Because GPU L2 is shared rather than explicitly pinned, weight residency cannot be assumed across all 20 steps, and the narrow workload does not saturate the GPU’s SIMT datapath. The KR260 FPGA has lower raw arithmetic throughput but ex-poses programmable on-chip BRAM/URAM storage [1]. EdgeDAE exploits this by storing all DAE weights, biases, quantization scales, LayerNorm parameters, time encodings, and DDPM schedule con-stants on chip. For the compact Octo DAE, this turns repeated memory-bound denoising into a deterministic compute pipeline, while the larger vision-language transformer remains on the GPU.

EdgeDAE addresses these limitations through a fully self-contained FPGA architecture with complete on-chip parameter storage, integrated random number generation within FPGA logic, and pipelined diffusion execution with pre-loaded constants enabling continuous execution without dynamic memory fetches. 4.2 GRNG Unit Design 

The GRNG unit supplies the fresh Gaussian noise vectors required by each DDPM step without host intervention. It uses four inde-pendent Xorshift128 streams, each with a dedicated 128-bit state, and feeds pairs of uniform samples into a Box-Muller transform. Complete state partitioning lets all four lanes advance without con-tention, producing four Gaussian samples per cycle after pipeline fill. Since the Octo action vector has 𝐷 𝑎 =28 elements, each timestep noise vector is generated in seven vector blocks and remains off the MLP-dominated critical path.

5 Experimental Results and Analysis 

We evaluate EdgeDAE along three axes: FPGA resource utilization, end-to-end latency and energy efficiency versus GPU baselines, and action prediction quality under different quantization strategies. The EdgeDAE diffusion action head is implemented on the KR260 FPGA platform at 250 MHz clock frequency. The complete Octo VLA system employs a heterogeneous architecture with Jetson Orin Nano in MAXN_SUPER mode, executing the vision trans-former encoder using PyTorch 2.3.0 with torch.compile optimiza-tion, while the KR260 accelerates the diffusion action head with quantized weights stored entirely on-chip. The non-diffusion GPU pipeline—language encoder, image encoder, and vision-language transformer—is compiled as a single torch.compile unit with 

reduce-overhead mode, enabling cross-module optimization. Both Octo-Small with ViT-S 384-dim backbone and Octo-Base with ViT-B 768-dim backbone are profiled using CUDA event timing over 200 iterations after 50 warmup iterations. The two platforms communi-cate via 1 Gbps Ethernet using UDP protocol to transfer the readout token—1 × 384 for Octo-Small and 1 × 768 for Octo-Base—with mea-sured latency of 70 𝜇 s. Baseline GPU performance metrics for RTX 4090 are obtained from the official Octo GitHub repository[17]. For the RTX 4090 power consumption, we reference the official NVIDIA product specifications [9], which rate the maximum Thermal De-sign Power at 450 W; we adopt 200 W as TDP upper bound for the energy efficiency comparison. 6 Conclusion 

EdgeDAE offloads the diffusion action head to FPGA while retain-ing vision-language processing on an edge GPU. INT8/NF4-g16 quantization keeps all weights on chip; NF4-g16 halves URAM us-age relative to INT8 while preserving action quality. Against the Jetson-only baseline, EdgeDAE cuts end-to-end latency by 52.5% for Octo-Small and 38.7% for Octo-Base, while delivering 17 .3× higher energy efficiency than an RTX 4090.
