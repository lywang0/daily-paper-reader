# HintKD: Hint Knowledge Distillation for Bandwidth-Constrained Cloud-Edge Inference 

Wanling Luo, Jiayi Zhang, Senior Member, IEEE , Bokai Xu, and Ziheng Liu 

Abstract —Collaborative inference between the edge cloud and user equipment (UE) is a promising paradigm for deploying large deep neural networks (DNNs) in 6G networks. However, existing cloud-edge inference and distillation schemes often require the real-time transmission of high-dimensional intermediate features or soft probability vectors, which imposes a substantial communi-cation burden on bandwidth-limited wireless links. To address this challenge, we propose HintKD, a bandwidth-constrained distillation framework for cloud-edge inference. The core idea is to compress the teacher’s guidance into compact discrete hints rather than transmit raw features directly. Specifically, a cloud-side teacher is first trained with a maximum conditional mutual information (MCMI) objective to preserve informative intra-class variations. Its latent representation is then mapped to a compact codebook through differentiable vector quantization. During deployment, the cloud transmits only a hint index, and the UE uses a lightweight FiLM-based adapter to modulate student features according to the received codeword. Experiments on the DeepSense 6G beam prediction task show that HintKD preserves competitive accuracy while reducing the inference-side payload from high-dimensional feature or logit transmission by 

O(Dt)/O(1) or O(C)/O(1) times. In addition, experiments on the MNIST dataset further verify the generality of the proposed dis-tillation method across different datasets and tasks. These results show that HintKD achieves a favorable accuracy-bandwidth trade-off by replacing high-dimensional feature or logit transmission with a compact discrete hint index. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

With the rapid proliferation of the Internet of Things (IoT) and the advent of 6G networks, deploying artificial intelligence (AI) at the network edge has become increasingly important [1]–[3]. However, user equipment (UE) is typically constrained by limited battery capacity, memory, and computing resources, which makes it difficult to deploy large-scale deep neural net-works (DNNs) directly on end devices [1], [2]. To address this challenge, existing research has mainly followed two directions: cloud-edge collaboration and on-device intelligence through model compression. Recent 6G network designs increasingly integrate edge-device collaboration with native artificial intelli-gence capabilities [4]. Cloud-edge collaboration reduces the computational burden on UEs by allowing resource-intensive processing to be sup-ported by the cloud or edge server [5], [6]. In this paradigm, the UE performs only lightweight local computation, while the remaining inference process relies on cloud-side assistance. Although such collaboration can effectively exploit powerful infrastructure, it also creates a strong dependence on wireless connectivity. In particular, when intermediate information must

As a result, the quality of service degrade sharply in poor channel conditions or coverage holes. At the physical layer, recent studies have investigated fluid-antenna-assisted integrated sensing and communications for unsourced random access as promising mechanisms for exploit-ing spatial diversity, supporting massive connectivity in future wireless networks [9], [10]. Compared with pure cloud-assisted inference, knowledge distillation (KD) offers a more attractive way to endow UEs with autonomous inference capability. In standard KD, alightweight student model learns from a stronger teacher model by matching its output distribution [11]. Subsequent studies extended this idea from logits to richer intermediate represen-tations, including feature hints, attention maps, and correlation-based relations, in order to improve the student’s generalization ability [12]–[14]. These advances have made KD an effective framework for transferring knowledge from large models to compact edge models. However, most existing KD methods are developed under offline or local settings, where the teacher and student are co-located or where communication is not a limiting factor. In a realistic wireless edge architecture, by contrast, the teacher is naturally deployed on the cloud or base station side, while the student runs on the UE. This physical separation fun-damentally changes the distillation problem: if the teacher’s guidance is transmitted as high-dimensional features or soft logits, the communication overhead can become prohibitive for bandwidth-constrained wireless links [15]. In particular, feature-based transmission scales with the teacher feature di-mension, and logits-based transmission scales with the number of classes, making both approaches inefficient for real-time cloud-edge inference. Related studies on deep CSI compression and feedback further demonstrate the importance of reducing the dimensionality of transmitted wireless representations in bandwidth-limited 6G systems [16]. To address this problem, we propose HintKD, a bandwidth-constrained distillation framework for cloud-edge inference. Instead of transmitting raw teacher features or full logit vectors, HintKD compresses the teacher’s guidance into a compact dis-crete hint. More specifically, a cloud-side teacher is first trained with a maximum conditional mutual information (MCMI) ob-jective so that its representation preserves informative intra-class variation. The resulting latent feature is then mapped into a compact codebook through differentiable vector quantization. In this way, remote teacher guidance is converted into a low-bit control signal suitable for bandwidth-limited edge intelligence. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. First, we develop a cloud-edge distillation frame-work that replaces high-dimensional teacher transmission with compact discrete hint signaling. Second, we integrate MCMI-based teacher learning, differentiable codebook design, and FiLM-based student adaptation into a unified architecture for bandwidth-constrained inference. Third, we analyze the com-munication complexity of the proposed framework and show that the inference-side payload is reduced from feature- or logit-level real-valued transmission to a low-bit discrete index. Finally, experiments on the DeepSense 6G beam prediction task demonstrate that HintKD preserves competitive predictive accuracy while achieving a more favorable accuracy-bandwidth trade-off than conventional Feature KD and Logits KD base-lines, and additional experiments on MNIST further verify the generality of the proposed method across different datasets and tasks. B. Cloud-Side: Contextual Vector Quantization 

This subsection details the vector quantization mechanism 

Q(zt; ψ) introduced in (3) designed for the cloud-side teacher. It takes the continuous teacher feature zt as input and outputs a discrete index. Although jointly optimized with the student during the offline training phase, this module is specifically engineered to compress the teacher’s high-dimensional features into compact codes for bandwidth-constrained transmission during deployment. To enable bandwidth-limited transmission, we map zt into a discrete codebook C = {ek}Kk=1 , where 

ek ∈ RDh represents the k-th latent prototype vector, and K

is the codebook size, inspired by vector quantized variational autoencoders (VQ-VAE) [19]. During training, we employ the Gumbel-Softmax relaxation to bridge the gradient flow. The soft probability vector p =[p1, . . . , p K ]⊤ ∈ ∆K−1 is computed as:

This ensures that the transmission payload is strictly bounded by the codebook entropy ⌈log 2 K⌉ bits, achieving a constant commu-nication overhead O(1) regardless of the feature dimension Dt.In stark contrast, traditional Feature KD requires transmitting the raw latent vector with a complexity of O(Dt), while Logits KD scales with the number of classes as O(C). Since typically O(1) < O(C) < O(Dt) , our method establishes an exponential reduction in transmission payload. It is crucial to distinguish the transmission payload between phases. During the offline joint training, to preserve differen-tiability for back-propagation, the Cloud-side transmits the soft probability vector p ∈ RK (4) rather than the discrete index. Even in this high-precision mode, our method remains efficient. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed HintKD frame-work, we conduct experiments on both the DeepSense 6G dataset and the MNIST dataset. Among them, DeepSense 6G Scenario 9 is used as the main beam prediction task to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method under bandwidth-constrained cloud-edge inference, while MNIST is further introduced to validate the generality of the proposed distillation method across different data modalities and classification tasks [21], [22]. Specifically, Scenario 9 corresponds to a radar-based beam prediction problem, whereas MNIST is a standard handwritten digit classification benchmark. Therefore, the joint evaluation on these two datasets helps demonstrate that HintKD is not limited to a specific sensing modality, but can also maintain effectiveness on conventional image classification tasks. The detailed experimental settings are summarized in Table I, including the evaluated datasets, input dimensions, backbone configurations, hint bandwidth, codebook size, and training hyperparameters. Fig. 2 compares different methods in terms of Accuracy, CMI, and LL. It can be observed that the teacher trained with the MCMI objective consistently achieves a higher CMI value, while the MLL-trained teacher exhibits a lower LL value. This indicates that the two teacher objectives emphasize different representation properties. More importantly, such differences are propagated to the student side and further affect the final distillation performance, showing that the teacher objective plays an important role in the proposed hint-based cloud-edge distillation framework. Table II reports the comprehensive results on DeepSense 6G Scenario 9. The offline student achieves 47.12% Top-1 accuracy and 96.93% Top-5 accuracy. Under the default 4-bit setting, HintKD with an MCMI-trained teacher reaches 49.53% Top-1 accuracy and 97.65% Top-5 accuracy, which improves the Top-1 accuracy by 2.41 percentage points over the offline baseline. In contrast, the 4-bit HintKD variant with an MLL-trained teacher achieves 46.95% Top-1 accuracy, which is lower than the MCMI-based counterpart. This shows that, for Scenario 9, introducing MCMI into teacher training can provide more informative guidance for the student and thus lead to better final performance. We further analyze the effect of hint bandwidth on Scenario 9. When the hint bandwidth is reduced from 4 bits to 2 bits, the Top-1 accuracy decreases to 45.44%, indicating that an overly small codebook cannot preserve sufficient discriminative information. When the bandwidth is increased from 4 bits to 6 bits, the Top-1 accuracy becomes 47.07%, which still does not surpass the 4-bit setting. Therefore, the 4-bit configuration provides the best trade-off between compact representation and predictive performance on Scenario 9. In addition to predictive accuracy, HintKD also exhibits clear communication advantages. On Scenario 9, Feature KD and Logits KD require 2688 bits and 1056 bits per sample during inference, respectively, whereas HintKD only needs 4 bits in the default setting. This corresponds to compression ratios of 672× and 264× relative to Feature KD and Logits KD, respectively. Even during training, the transmission cost of HintKD remains lower than that of Feature KD. These results show that the proposed method can significantly re-duce communication overhead while preserving competitive beam prediction performance, which is particularly suitable for bandwidth-constrained cloud-edge inference scenarios. The communication overhead comparison is also illustrated in Fig. 3. To further verify the generality of the proposed distillation framework, we additionally conduct experiments on the MNIST dataset. Compared with the radar-based beam prediction task in Scenario 9, MNIST is a conventional handwritten digit recognition task, which differs significantly in data form, se-mantic structure, and application background. Therefore, the MNIST results are used to demonstrate that HintKD is not only effective for wireless sensing aided beam prediction, but also applicable to standard image classification tasks. As shown in Table II, the offline student on MNIST achieves 99.05% Top-1 accuracy. Under the 4-bit setting, HintKD with an MLL-trained teacher reaches 99.17% Top-1 accuracy, while the MCMI-based HintKD reaches 99.06%. At the same time, the Top-5 accuracy remains stable at 99.97%, indicating that the proposed method can maintain highly competitive classification perfor-mance even with an extremely low-bandwidth hint transmission mechanism. From the perspective of communication overhead, the advantage of HintKD is still evident on MNIST. In the 4-bit setting, its training-side cost is 512 bits and inference-side cost is only 4 bits, which is far lower than Feature KD and also much lower than the inference overhead of Logits KD. Combined with the results on Scenario 9, these experiments show that HintKD consistently achieves a favorable accuracy-bandwidth trade-off across different datasets and tasks. This paper proposed HintKD, a cloud-edge distillation frame-work that replaces high-dimensional teacher transmissions with compact discrete hints. By combining an MCMI-trained teacher, differentiable codebook learning, and FiLM-based hint adaptation on the UE, the proposed method converts remote teacher guidance into a low-bit control signal. Experiments on DeepSense 6G and MNIST show that HintKD preserves competitive accuracy while reducing the inference-side pay-load to only a few bits per sample. These results suggest that, for bandwidth-constrained edge intelligence, transmitting structured guidance can be considerably more efficient than transmitting raw features or full logit vectors.
